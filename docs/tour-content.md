## Cavite

### 1. Aguinaldo Shrine

**History:** The house was first built in 1845 of wood and thatch, and rebuilt in 1849. On June 12, 1898, Philippine independence from Spain was proclaimed from a window of its main hall. The declaration was read by its author, Ambrosio Rianzares Bautista. The Philippine flag was formally unfurled, and a marching band played the music of the national anthem for the first time. Emilio Aguinaldo enlarged the house from 1919 to 1921 and added the famous balcony then, so the balcony was not yet there in 1898. He donated the house to the government on June 12, 1963, and it was declared a National Shrine in 1964.

**Cultural Significance:** This is where the Philippines declared itself a free nation, which is why Independence Day is celebrated every June 12. Aguinaldo, the first president, is buried in a marble tomb in the garden behind the house. The National Historical Commission of the Philippines (NHCP) keeps a museum on the ground floor.

- **Sources:** [Wikipedia: Aguinaldo Shrine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aguinaldo_Shrine)

### 2. Corregidor Island

**History:** Corregidor is a tadpole-shaped island nicknamed "The Rock." Under Spain it was a fortress and a signal post that warned Manila of approaching enemy ships. The Americans fortified it and set up Fort Mills there in 1908. During World War II the island became the temporary seat of the Philippine government: Manuel L. Quezon and Sergio Osmeña were inaugurated there as president and vice president on December 30, 1941. Gen. Douglas MacArthur used it as Allied headquarters until March 11, 1942. The defenders, under Lt. Gen. Jonathan Wainwright, surrendered to Japanese forces on May 6, 1942. American and Filipino forces took the island back in February 1945.

**Cultural Significance:** Corregidor is remembered for the Filipino and American soldiers who defended it in World War II. The Pacific War Memorial on the island, built by the United States government and completed in 1968, honors them. Today it is a major historical site and is considered part of Cavite City.

- **Sources:** [Wikipedia: Corregidor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corregidor)

### 3. Bonifacio Trial House

**History:** The house was built in 1889. In May 1897, during the revolution against Spain, Katipunan leader Andrés Bonifacio and his brother Procopio were tried here by a court-martial headed by Brig. Gen. Mariano Noriel. On May 5 the brothers were charged with treason and sedition, and on May 6 they were sentenced to death. On May 10, 1897, they were taken to the mountains of Maragondon and executed. The house was declared a National Historical Landmark on June 4, 1997, and it opened as a museum, the Museo ng Paglilitis ni Andres Bonifacio, on November 28, 2014.

**Cultural Significance:** The trial is a difficult chapter of the revolution, because Bonifacio was tried by fellow revolutionaries, and his death is still debated today. The museum has life-size dioramas of the trial scene, so visitors can see what happened in these rooms and think about it for themselves.

## Laguna

### 4. Rizal Shrine

**History:** José Rizal was born here on June 19, 1861. His father took two years to build the original house, a bahay na bato (stone house) like the homes of well-off Filipino families of that time. Spanish authorities took the house from the family in 1891. It was later sold, and it was destroyed in World War II. The government bought what was left, and President Elpidio Quirino ordered the house rebuilt. Filipino schoolchildren gave most of the money, Juan Nakpil was the supervising architect, and the new house was inaugurated on June 19, 1950.

**Cultural Significance:** The shrine lets visitors see how the national hero lived as a child. In 2009 its walls were repainted green to highlight the meaning of Rizal's surname. The remains of Rizal's parents, Francisco Mercado and Teodora Alonso, are kept in the grounds, and a statue made in 1996 shows the young Rizal with his dog. The house is a National Shrine.

- **Check:** The year of President Quirino's order (Executive Order No. 145). Wikipedia's text says 1949, but its reference dates the order June 19, 1948. The year is left out.

### 5. Pagsanjan Falls

**History:** The boat ride to the falls has drawn visitors since Spanish colonial times, and the oldest written account of it that survives is from 1894. The falls and the gorge around them were declared a national park on March 29, 1939. The old name comes from the town of Pagsanjan, where the boat ride begins, but the main falls are inside the town of Cavinti. They can also be reached from the top by a short hike from Cavinti.

**Cultural Significance:** A local legend tells of two brothers, Balubad and Magdapio. During a long drought, Magdapio struck the cliffs with his cane, and a spring welled up where the cane fell. That spring became the falls, which is why their local name is Magdapio Falls. The boat ride, called "Shooting the Rapids," depends on the skill of the local boatmen. A raft can take visitors through the falling water to Devil's Cave behind the falls.

- **Sources:** [Wikipedia: Pagsanjan Falls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pagsanjan_Falls)

### 6. Nagcarlan Underground Cemetery

**History:** The cemetery was built in 1845 under the Franciscan priest Fr. Vicente Velloc. It is circular, covers about one hectare, and is surrounded by eight-sided walls of red brick with iron grills. Funeral Masses were held in the chapel, and the crypt lies 15 feet below it. During the revolution against Spain, Katipunan leaders met here, and Pedro Paterno and Gen. Severino Taiño planned the Pact of Biak-na-Bato here. It later served as a hideout for Filipino leaders in the Philippine–American War and for guerrillas in World War II.

**Cultural Significance:** It is known as the only Catholic cemetery in the country with an underground crypt. It shows the burial customs of Spanish times, when friars and leading families were given a place of honor, and it is tied to three wars for freedom. It is a National Historical Landmark managed by the National Historical Commission of the Philippines (NHCP), while the property still belongs to the Diocese of San Pablo.

- **Check:** The year it became a National Historical Landmark (1973 or 1981), as noted in the midterm. The year is left out.

## Batangas

### 7. Taal Heritage Town

**History:** Taal was founded by Augustinian friars in 1572, and the old town stood beside Taal Lake. In 1754, the greatest recorded eruption of Taal Volcano destroyed the old town, and the people moved to the present site on a hill facing Balayan Bay. There, families built the houses that still line the streets today: the town has hundreds of heritage buildings from Spanish times. The Agoncillo house, now a museum, was built in the 1770s by the grandparents of Marcela Agoncillo.

**Cultural Significance:** The town proper is a National Historical Landmark. Taal was home to heroes of the revolution: Marcela Agoncillo, known as the "Mother of the Philippine Flag"; Felipe Agoncillo, a diplomat; and Gliceria de Villavicencio, whom Gen. Emilio Aguinaldo named "godmother of the revolutionary forces." Taal is also called the balisong and barong Tagalog capital of the Philippines, for its knife-making and embroidery.

### 8. Basilica of St. Martin de Tours

**History:** The first church was begun in 1575 near the shore of Taal Lake. It was destroyed, along with the old town, by the 1754 eruption of Taal Volcano. After the town moved to its present site, a new church was begun in 1755, but a strong earthquake damaged it in 1852. Work on the present church began in 1856 under the Spanish architect Luciano Oliver. It was inaugurated in 1865 while still unfinished, and completed in 1878. It was declared a minor basilica on December 8, 1954, and a National Historical Landmark in 1974.

**Cultural Significance:** The basilica is considered the largest church building in the country. Its patron, St. Martin of Tours, is also the patron saint of Taal, and the town celebrates his feast every November 11. The church shows how the people of Taal rebuilt, again and again, after an eruption and an earthquake.

### 9. Taal Volcano

**History:** Taal is a large caldera, a huge bowl left by ancient eruptions, that is now filled by Taal Lake. It is the second most active volcano in the country, with 39 confirmed eruptions in recorded history. Its greatest recorded eruption began on May 15, 1754, lasted for more than six months, and forced the towns of Tanauan, Taal, Lipa, and Sala to move. An eruption in 1911 killed more than a thousand people. On January 12, 2020, Taal erupted again, and 39 people died.

**Cultural Significance:** Volcano Island has a lake in its crater, and inside that lake is a small rocky island called Vulcan Point: an island in a lake, on an island in a lake. Scientists named Taal a Decade Volcano, one that deserves close study to prevent disasters. Permanent settlement on Volcano Island is prohibited by PHIVOLCS. Taal Lake is also home to the tawilis, a sardine that adapted to fresh water.

## Rizal

### 10. Hinulugang Taktak

**History:** Folk tradition says that villagers found a large bell too loud, so it was dropped (hinulog) into the falls in the 15th or 16th century. The falls are 21.5 meters high. They were already a popular place for outings by 1929, when composer German San Jose mentioned them in his song "Antipolo." The area was reserved for recreation in 1952. Beginning in the 1960s the water slowly became polluted, and rehabilitation work started in 1991. After a year of rehabilitation, the park reopened to the public on February 13, 2020.

**Cultural Significance:** Generations of visitors to Antipolo have stopped at the falls, which are less than two kilometers from Antipolo Cathedral. The story of Hinulugang Taktak is also a lesson in caring for nature: a famous waterfall became polluted, and it took many years of work to bring it back. The park is managed by the Department of Environment and Natural Resources (DENR) and the Rizal provincial government.

### 11. Antipolo Cathedral

**History:** The church was built to house the image of Our Lady of Peace and Good Voyage, which was brought to the Philippines by Governor-General Juan Niño de Tabora. Jesuit priests ran the church from 1591 to 1768. In 1945, during World War II, the old church was destroyed by bombing. A new church designed by architect Jose L. de Ocampo was built from 1948 to 1954, and it was declared a national shrine in 1954. It became a cathedral on June 25, 1983.

**Cultural Significance:** The cathedral enshrines a dark image of the Virgin Mary, called a Black Madonna, whose title asks for peace and a safe journey. Pilgrims come especially during the pilgrimage season, from May to July. In 2024 it was declared the first international shrine in the Philippines and in Southeast Asia.

### 12. Masungi Georeserve

**History:** Masungi sits on limestone peaks in the southern part of the Sierra Madre mountain range, in Baras, Rizal. In 1993 the Department of Environment and Natural Resources (DENR) proposed making Masungi Rock a strict nature reserve and wildlife sanctuary. A conservation project began in 1996 as a joint venture between the DENR and a private company, but work was slow because of conflicts with logging and quarrying companies. The park opened to visitors in 2015, the same year the Masungi Georeserve Foundation was set up. In 2017 a project began to restore 2,700 hectares of damaged watershed land around it.

**Cultural Significance:** Masungi is home to about 400 kinds of plants and animals, including the Northern Luzon giant cloud rat and the Philippine serpent eagle, and more than 100,000 native trees grow around the park. Its trail features have Filipino names: Sapot is a platform of netting above the rocks, Duyan is a giant rope hammock, and Tatay is the tallest peak. Masungi is an example of ecotourism, where visiting a place is tied to protecting it. In 2022 its foundation won a United Nations Sustainable Development Goals Action Award.

- **Check:** Masungi's agreements with the DENR are in dispute (Wikipedia reports cancellations in 2024 and 2025). Before presenting, check whether the park is still open to visitors. The dispute is left out of the text.

### 13. Kamay ni Hesus Shrine

### 14. Pahiyas Festival

**History:** Pahiyas is a thanksgiving for the harvest, held every May 15 in honor of San Isidro Labrador (St. Isidore the Laborer), the patron saint of farmers. The day begins with an early-morning Mass at Lucban Church. Families then decorate the fronts of their houses with fruits, vegetables, rice stalks, handicrafts, and kiping. The houses are judged, and the best one is named the winner. Today the celebration lasts a whole week, starting every May 15.

**Cultural Significance:** In Pahiyas the harvest itself becomes the decoration, which shows how a farming town says thank you for its food. Kiping, the colorful wafers made from rice, can be eaten afterward, grilled or fried. Lucban is also called the Longganisa Capital of Southern Tagalog, and its sausages decorate houses and market stalls during the festival.

### 15. Quezon Provincial Capitol

**History:** The province used to be called Tayabas. The capitol was built in the early 1900s, during the American period, when it was designed by American architect William Parsons and later expanded by Filipino architect Juan Arellano. On September 7, 1946, a law renamed the province Quezon, in honor of Manuel L. Quezon, the Commonwealth president who came from Baler, which was then part of the province. The building kept its old name on the front.

**Cultural Significance:** The capitol is still the seat of the provincial government in Lucena, the capital of Quezon. Its front, which still reads "Tayabas Provincial Capitol," is a reminder of the province's older name. The building also shows the work of an American and a Filipino architect side by side.
